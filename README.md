### Which one should you actually use?

**If you want XRP today and the amount is small**, use a card. You pay for the convenience, roughly 1–5% depending on region and provider, and the coins normally land within minutes. For a $50 test purchase, a 4% fee is two dollars. Annoying, not fatal.

**If the amount is meaningful**, do the boring thing: bank transfer, then buy on the spot market. You skip the card processing fee entirely, the spot fee at the base tier is 0.10%, and the only cost you pay for the slower route is waiting on your bank's settlement. On a $5,000 purchase, that difference is worth more than the coffee you'd buy while waiting.

**If you already hold USDT or ETH**, don't route through fiat at all. The Convert tool swaps into XRP in one step at a small spread, and there's no order book to read.

**If your local bank blocks crypto transactions**, C2C is the fallback. Gate holds the crypto in escrow until the seller releases it, but you are trading against a person, not a rate sheet, so compare the seller's quoted price against the live market before you accept it.

## Step 3: Placing the XRP order

There are two doors here, and they suit different people.

**The buy widget** asks for a fiat amount — say $200 — and shows you how much XRP that gets. Choose the payment method, confirm the card if it's your first time, and pass the bank's one-time password check. One quirk worth knowing: the quote is only good for about a minute. If the clock runs out, refresh it and take the new price rather than clicking through on a stale number.

**The spot market** is the one to use if you care about the price you get. Go to the XRP pair — XRP/USDT is the deepest, though XRP/BTC and XRP/USDC are also listed — and pick an order type:

- **Market order**: fills immediately at whatever the book offers. Fast, and normally fine for small orders on a liquid pair.
- **Limit order**: you name your price and wait. It only fills when someone meets it, which means it might never fill. If you're buying in a quiet market, this is how you avoid paying the spread.
- **Take-profit / stop-loss**: available on the spot market for people who already have a plan.

Type the amount, hit buy, and the XRP appears in your spot wallet. If you're new to order books, the market order is the one to learn on — you can experiment with limit pricing once you've watched a fill happen.

## Step 4: Confirm it landed

Check your spot wallet before you do anything else. The balance should match, minus fees. Two things to verify:

- The **order history** shows the fill price and the fee charged, so you can see exactly what 0.10% looked like in dollars.
- The **total** matches what you expected. If you bought via card, remember that the fee was baked into the price you were quoted, not deducted from the XRP amount.

If the XRP hasn't shown up after a card purchase that took your money, don't buy again — open a support ticket and wait. Duplicate orders are a real problem, and they're usually user-generated.

## Step 5: Keep it on the exchange, or move it?

Leaving XRP on an exchange is convenient: you can sell, convert or trade it instantly. It also means you don't hold the keys. Moving it to your own wallet — a software wallet with XRP Ledger support, or a hardware wallet — puts full control in your hands and removes exchange counterparty risk. The trade-off is that you now own the responsibility for the seed phrase.

If you do withdraw, three details matter more than anything else in this guide:

> **The destination tag is not decoration.** Exchanges pool customer XRP in shared wallets and identify who owns what by a numeric tag attached to each deposit. Send XRP to an exchange address without its tag and the coins arrive correctly addressed to nobody. Recovery means a support ticket and, usually, weeks. Depositing *to* an exchange: copy the address and the tag, every time. Withdrawing to your own private wallet: no tag needed.

- **Network choice.** Send on the XRP Ledger, not on some wrapper token on another chain. If you hold XRP wrapped on Ethereum or BNB Chain, that's a different asset with a contract address, and it needs the matching network to move.
- **The base reserve.** The XRP Ledger locks a small base reserve in every funded account — currently 1 XRP, down from the 10 XRP older guides still quote. That's not a fee, and it stays yours if you ever close the account.
- **Test first.** For any withdrawal, send a small amount, confirm it arrives, then send the rest. It costs one extra network fee and eliminates an entire category of regret.

## How much XRP does $100 buy?

This is a moving target, so treat any fixed number you read as a snapshot. The arithmetic is simple: dollars divided by the XRP price, minus fees.

If XRP is quoted at $1.20 and you buy $100 on a card with a 3.5% fee, you're effectively working with about $96.50 of buying power, which lands roughly 80 XRP. The same $100 by bank transfer into a spot limit order keeps essentially all of it, minus 0.10%, and gets you closer to 83 XRP. Same money, meaningfully different outcome — and that's before price movement between the two paths.

Minimum purchase amounts are set per payment channel and per region, and the buy widget shows yours before you confirm. Don't assume the $10 you saw in someone's screenshot applies to your country.

## Beginner mistakes worth avoiding

**Sending XRP to the wrong network.** XRP exists as a native ledger asset and as wrapped versions on other chains. Native XRP goes on the XRP Ledger. Get this wrong and the coins are gone.

**Forgetting the tag.** Covered above because it's the single most common expensive mistake for XRP specifically. Copy the tag whenever the receiving wallet shows one.

**Paying card fees on a large purchase.** Convenience is worth 1–5% on a test buy. On $10,000 it's a month's rent in some cities.

**Buying on a credit card you won't clear.** Crypto prices fall as well as rise, and card interest on a losing position is a bad combination.

**Skipping 2FA until after the deposit.** Accounts get compromised between the deposit and the withdrawal, not before.

**Buying without understanding what you own.** XRP the digital asset and Ripple the company are related but distinct, and that confusion shows up constantly in comment sections. Long-running US legal uncertainty around XRP has been resolved and the coin is widely listed again, but that's a reason to read, not a reason to skip the reading.

## FAQ

**Is it legal to buy XRP?**
In most major markets, yes, and the US regulatory picture that once caused delistings has been settled, with spot XRP ETFs now trading on US exchanges. Regional rules still differ, and Gate restricts some services by location. If your country is unsupported, you won't see the purchase option at all.

**Do I need a wallet?**
No, not to start. You can hold XRP on the exchange indefinitely. A private wallet matters when the amount is large enough that exchange counterparty risk bothers you.

**Can I buy XRP with $10?**
Usually, but it depends on the channel and the country. Card minimums are often the flexible, expensive option; bank transfers set higher floors. The order screen shows your minimum.

**How long does verification take?**
Often minutes, occasionally up to a day if the queue is long. Do it before you need it.

**Can I sell it back to cash?**
Yes. Sell XRP for USDT on the spot market, then withdraw through whatever fiat channel your region supports. Not every region has one, so check before you buy rather than after.

**Should I wait for a better price?**
Nobody knows. What you can control is the fee, the payment method and whether you understand the withdrawal rules. Those three decisions are worth more than most price timing attempts.
